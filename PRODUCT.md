# DormShare product context

## Register

product

## Users

NYU students looking for temporary access to everyday items, and students who want to lend or rent out items they own. The main jobs are finding an item, agreeing on terms, managing a rental, and recording its pickup and return.

Students need useful phone layouts while arranging an exchange. Desktop layouts should also support browsing, listing management, messages, and rental dashboards.

## Product purpose

Make borrowing, lending, and renting within the student community clear and convenient. Keep the item, daily price, dates, agreed terms, and next action easy to find. Payments are arranged between participants outside DormShare.

The product scope comes from [README.md](README.md) and [UX-DESIGN.md](UX-DESIGN.md).

## Brand personality

Practical, welcoming, and trustworthy. Use the university-wide NYU identity selected by the project owner. DormShare keeps its own name and identity.

The owner selected the light campus marketplace direction on November 18, 2026. It gives listings more space and uses violet for actions and selected navigation. See the [selected concept](docs/design/concepts/light-marketplace.png).

## Anti-references

- Avoid crowded portal navigation that takes space away from the student's task.
- Avoid promotional statistics, invented verification claims, and artificial urgency.
- Avoid replacing working forms and rental behavior merely to change their appearance.

## Design principles

1. Show the item and next action clearly.
2. Keep the same control and status language across journeys.
3. Make phone layouts complete and usable.
4. Explain pending work, errors, and recovery without losing entered data.
5. Preserve the existing service and state contracts during the visual rework.

## Accessibility and inclusion

Use WCAG 2.2 AA as the project target. This is a DormShare target, not a claim that the current app meets it. Include keyboard access, visible focus, meaningful labels, readable contrast, text zoom, reduced motion, and status cues that do not rely on color alone.
